# TASK
Segment the provided essay into distinct argument components:
- <Lead>...</Lead>: An introductory remark that often begins the essay. Typically comes in the form of a reiteration of the prompt, statistic, quotation, description, saluation, or other device to grab the reader’s attention and point toward the thesis.
- <Position>...</Position>: An opinion or thesis on the main question or prompt. The position will often disagree or agree with the prompt or provide an original thesis. 
- <Claim>...</Claim>: A statement or assertion that supports the position.
- <Counterclaim>...</Counterclaim>: A statement or assertion that opposes another claim or provides an opposing reason to the position. The counterclaim shows where the writer understands that there is another point of view that may be contrary to the writer’s own position. 
- <Rebuttal>...</Rebuttal>: A statement that refutes a counterclaim.
- <Evidence>...</Evidence>: Data, facts, or examples that support claims, counterclaims, or rebuttals.
- <Concluding Statement>...</Concluding Statement>: A statement that restates the claims and summarizes the argument. Typically occurs at the end of the essay.
- <Pad>...</Pad>: Any portion of the essay that does not add any value to the writer's argument, e.g. copying the prompt verbatim, repetitions of large parts of the essay verbatim, etc.

After each argument component, insert the corresponding markers, e.g. insert <Lead> at the start of a lead component and insert </Lead> at the end of a lead component.
